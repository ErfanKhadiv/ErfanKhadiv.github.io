Research: HBALS

A hybrid Bees Algorithm with adaptive local search, which I designed for the Traveling Salesman Problem. It was my bachelor's final project, and I am the sole author of the manuscript submitted to Evolutionary Intelligence (Springer).

Code, data and paper

The problem

Given a set of cities, find the shortest route that visits each one once and returns to the start. The problem is NP-hard, so for larger inputs the practical goal is a very good route found quickly, not a proof that it is the best.

The standard Bees Algorithm can converge slowly or stall in local optima. My question was whether adaptive neighbourhood control and local search can improve it while keeping the population exploring.

Reading the animation

  • White dot: a city.
  • Yellow dot: a bee, flying along the best route found so far.
  • Line: the best route so far. The counters show the iteration and its length.

In the algorithm itself, each bee is a candidate route. Bees search close to the best routes they know, while scouts try random new ones. The animation runs the real search in the background and uses the yellow dots to let you follow the best route.

How HBALS works

  1. StartA population of 20 routes: half built with nearest-neighbour, half random.
  2. RankAll routes are sorted by total length.
  3. Elite sitesThe best 3 routes each spawn 8 variants, improved with 2-opt and sometimes 3-opt.
  4. Selected sitesThe next best, up to 12 sites in all, spawn 4 variants each with a lighter search.
  5. ScoutsNew random routes refill the population so the search keeps exploring.
  6. Adapt and stopThe search neighbourhood shrinks while progress continues and widens after 3 stalled iterations. It stops after 20 iterations without improvement.

20 independent runs per algorithm, 300 iterations at most. Baselines: Bees Algorithm, Ant Colony Optimization, Genetic Algorithm and Particle Swarm Optimization. A ✓ marks a best tour equal to the best-known one.

A Friedman test shows a significant difference between the algorithms (p < 0.001). Against the Bees Algorithm and the Genetic Algorithm the gap is significant (Nemenyi p = 0.009 and 0.0001). Against Ant Colony and Particle Swarm it is not (p = 0.809 and 0.183), because only six instances were tested. Some published Bees Algorithm methods come closer to the best-known tours on the larger instances. The full implementation, datasets and results are open source under the MIT licence.
